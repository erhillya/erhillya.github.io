Attractions in Busan

Busan is a city of remarkable variety. During my time there, I got to explore colorful hillside villages, stunning coastal beaches, and bustling markets, all within easy reach of each other. I made a point of seeing as much as I could. Below are the places that left the strongest impression on me.

감천문화마을 · Gamcheon Culture Village

Gamcheon Culture Village is a must-visit destination, especially for photography enthusiasts. Colorful homes are stacked up a hillside in a way that feels almost like a painting come to life. Beyond the scenery, the village offers a charming scavenger hunt: follow the clues and collect stamps as you wind through the narrow alleys. Allow yourself to get a little lost; that is half the fun.

Emily's tip: Pick up the scavenger hunt map at the entrance for fun memories and a lovely keepsake.

해운대해수욕장 · Haeundae Beach

Haeundae is Busan's most famous beach, and it earns its reputation. The stretch of sand is beautiful, and the surrounding area is lively with cafés and restaurants. For an even more breathtaking experience, take the sky train out to 청사포 (Cheongsapo), a quieter seaside spot with sweeping ocean views and dreamy lighthouses.

Emily's tip: Book your tickets for the train beforehand to ensure you get a spot, especially if you are going with a group.

광안리 해수욕장 · Gwangalli Beach

Gwangalli Beach sparkles with life at night. The iconic 광안대교 (Gwangan Bridge) is lit up spectacularly after dark, and the beachfront fills with people enjoying the atmosphere. If you time your visit right, you may catch one of the drone shows that are held over the water, a genuinely magical experience that I was lucky enough to witness.

Emily's tip: Learn from my mistake and check which area of the beach has the best view of the drone show beforehand.

남포동 · Nampo-dong

Nampo-dong is one of Busan's most vibrant and beloved neighborhoods, offering something for every kind of traveler. The area is best known for the famous 자갈치시장 (Jagalchi Fish Market), one of the largest fish markets in Korea, where vendors sell live seafood straight from the tank and prepare it fresh for you on the spot. Not for the faint of heart, but certainly an unforgettable, and delicious, experience.

Beyond the market, Nampo-dong is a fantastic place to spend an evening. 남포길 (Nampo-gil) is lined with street food stalls (for more about the food, see Food). The area also has plenty of shopping and restaurants to explore. After dark, the neighborhood comes alive with vibrant light displays that make for a wonderful atmosphere to stroll through.

Emily's tip: Check out the docks around Jagalchi Market at night and you may happen upon trot-singing buskers and groups of dancing 아줌마 (ajumma — roughly translated as 'aunts,' though the term carries a lot more warmth and cultural meaning than that). It's truly a charming sight.
