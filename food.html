Food in Busan

If there is one thing I want you to take away from this site, it is that the food in Busan is extraordinary. I was lucky enough to be guided by my close friend 지홍 (Jihong), a chef from Busan with a remarkable network of fellow chefs and restaurant owners, which meant I got to experience places I never would have found on my own. Whether you are grabbing street food on a sunny morning or settling in for a long evening of food and drinks, Busan's food scene will not disappoint.

Casual Dining

남포동 길거리 음식 · Nampo-dong Street Food

Nampo-dong is one of Busan's most beloved neighborhoods, and on a bright morning it is an absolute joy to explore. Street food vendors are scattered throughout the area with an incredible variety of options; I had 닭꼬치 (dak-kkochi — chicken skewers), 떡볶이 (tteokbokki — spicy rice cakes), and 호떡 (hotteok — sweet filled pancakes), and every single bite was delicious. It is honestly some of the best street food I have ever eaten.

What struck me most was how safe, clean, and welcoming the whole area felt. Beyond the food, Nampo-dong is also a fantastic spot for shopping, and it sits right next to 자갈치시장 (Jagalchi Fish Market), making it easy to combine both into one outing. Whether you are there for breakfast or a evening snack, do not skip this street.

사철밀면 · Sacheol Milmyeon

I have been craving this bowl of 비빔면 (bibimmyeon) ever since I left Busan, and I mean that wholeheartedly. 사철밀면 serves what I genuinely believe is the best bibimmyeon I have ever eaten. The flavors are bold and perfectly balanced, and the portion is enormous for a price of around 6 USD. My friends also raved about their 냉면 (naengmyeon, cold noodles).

The 왕만두 (wangmandu — large dumplings) are equally wonderful: soft, generously filled, and incredibly satisfying. 밀면 (milmyeon) is a Busan specialty that you simply must try while you are in the city, and this warm, relaxed little spot is the perfect place to do it. The older woman who served us was so warm and engaging, the kind of hospitality that makes a meal truly memorable.

Nights Out

심야식당구미 · Simya Sikdang Gumi

This intimate little restaurant was one of the highlights of my entire time in Busan. Owned by a friend of Jihong's, it carries that rare quality of a place that genuinely cares about its guests. The 회 (hwe, raw fish) is extraordinary — impossibly fresh and served in generous portions. Unlike Japanese-style sashimi, Korean 회 comes with an array of sauces and optional additions that transform each bite into its own experience, making the way you eat it just as enjoyable as the fish itself. Paired with an excellent selection of drinks, it was truly remarkable.

Despite being on the smaller side, the space feels wonderfully intimate and private, with a cool, luxurious atmosphere that makes the evening feel special. The surrounding neighborhood is one of Busan's most exciting for nightlife, and true to Jihong's word, it is quite the area for people-watching. An unforgettable evening from start to finish.

끄티집 광안리 · Kkeutijib Gwangalli

끄티집 is a 요리주점 — a distinctly Korean type of establishment where drinking and eating are completely inseparable, as they should be according to Korean drinking culture. Also owned by a contact of Jihong's, this lively Gwangalli spot was our second stop of the evening, and it did not disappoint. We ordered 소고기말이찜 (steamed rolled beef), 해물전 (seafood pancake), and washed it all down with 수주 (soju) and 막걸리 (makgeolli). Every dish was absolutely delicious!

The atmosphere strikes a wonderful balance: the room itself is lively and energetic, yet our table still felt like its own intimate little world. We ended up staying for hours, caught up in the food, the drinks, and the conversation. If you want to experience Korean drinking culture at its most genuine and enjoyable, this is the place.
